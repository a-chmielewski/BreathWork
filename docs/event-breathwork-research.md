# Event-inspired breathwork research

Researched 7 September 2026. Scope: product ideas inspired by Amy Argyle's Pure Heart event; app inventory and implementation are assessed separately.

## What the event actually specifies

The event describes movement/stretching, lying down and grounding, setting an intention, cycles of **three different breath patterns**, facilitator-provided massage/tapping, and a closing mini sound bath. It suggests bringing a journal. The page **does not name the patterns, their cadence, intensity, or duration**. Three different patterns must not be confused with one three-part breath. [Pure Heart event](https://www.pureheartcollective.com/event-details/breathwork-soundbath-with-amy-argyle-2026-06-30-19-00)

Her studio bio describes verbal guidance, a curated playlist, live instruments, and touch with permission. Neither that bio nor her Healing Bus practitioner page supplies the missing protocols. An exact replication would require obtaining her session outline directly; this research did not contact her. [Evoken bio](https://www.evoken.co/amyargyle), [Healing Bus bio](https://healingbus.com/team/amy-argyle)

## Recommended additions

These are product proposals, not claims that the event uses these exercises or that the complete sequence has been clinically tested.

| Addition | Proposed experience | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guided session with several stages | Optional gentle movement, grounding/intention, one or more comfortable breathing exercises, natural breathing with sound, then optional reflection. Offer a short and a longer version. | Inspired by the event structure, not a reconstruction of its protocols. |
| Gentle belly-breathing introduction | Teach comfortable posture and relaxed breathing with a hand on the abdomen; start by observing natural breathing; offer pacing only if useful. | NHS guidance supports gentle, comfortable breathing without forcing depth, with optional counting. [NHS stress breathing](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/) |
| Comfortable longer exhale | Add a no-hold option with a slightly longer exhale and adjustable timing. For example, 4 in / 6 out is a product preset to try, not a universal prescription or exact ratio established by the cited page. | Guy's and St Thomas' teaches smooth abdominal breathing and making the out-breath slower. [Abdominal breathing](https://www.guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/health-information/abdominal-breathing) |
| Optional humming exhale | Comfortable nasal inhale, soft hum on the exhale, natural pitch, no vocal strain. Provide a silent alternative. | Cambridge University Hospitals teaches this mechanism in **voice therapy** and advises stopping for discomfort/tension. It does not establish stress treatment, trauma release, or a special vagal benefit. [CUH humming exercises](https://www.cuh.nhs.uk/patient-information/humming-exercises/) |
| Sound-rest stage | Optional licensed/original bowl or ambient recording, separate voice and music volume, fade into ordinary breathing without a pacing target. | An experiential feature inspired by the event. No special-frequency or sound-healing efficacy claim is warranted by the sources reviewed. |

Suggested first session: **15-minute Breath & Sound** — 2 minutes arrive/ground, 5 minutes gentle breathing, 3 minutes comfortable longer exhale, 4 minutes sound-rest with natural breathing, 1 minute return/reflection. These timings are an illustrative content design requiring usability and instructor review, not a medical protocol. A user should be able to skip stages or continue natural breathing at any point.

## Intensive breathwork is a separate product decision

Do not infer that this event uses connected, holotropic, or prolonged fast breathing. If considering those methods independently, do not present them as another interchangeable beginner timer preset. Cleveland Clinic describes holotropic practice as rapid/deep circular breathing, normally with trained facilitation and people monitoring participants, and lists health conditions requiring avoidance or medical discussion. [Cleveland Clinic on holotropic practice](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/should-you-try-holotropic-breathwork-heres-what-it-does)

Overbreathing can lower blood carbon dioxide and produce lightheadedness, tingling, muscle spasms, and other symptoms. These should not be marketed as proof of emotional release or encouraged as a goal. [Cleveland Clinic hyperventilation](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/hyperventilation)

Product implication: prioritize gentle self-guided sessions. Any future intensive offering needs a separately designed, qualified facilitator-led experience and method-specific eligibility and response procedures. Do not imply that audio, animation, or a disclaimer replaces live observation.

## Experience and language recommendations

- Make the complete session visible before starting: stages, duration, intensity, holds/no holds, sound/voice availability, and comfortable posture.
- Support eyes-open and eyes-closed participation, a quiet mode, captions/text prompts, gentle transitions, and an always-available return to natural breathing.
- Use choice-based instructions: invite users to adjust or skip, without framing completion or stronger sensations as achievement.
- Use optional intention and before/after feeling check-ins for reflection; avoid diagnosing mood or claiming treatment from those ratings.
- Describe expected experience plainly: relaxation, practicing breath awareness, taking time to settle. Avoid claims of clearing energy, releasing stored trauma, guaranteed nervous-system regulation, or healing a condition.

NCCIH considers relaxation practices generally safe for healthy people but notes occasional negative experiences, including increased anxiety, and limited evidence for some condition-specific benefits. That supports modest wording and usable exits rather than universal safety or treatment claims. [NCCIH relaxation techniques](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/relaxation-techniques-what-you-need-to-know)

## Uncertainty to retain

The event's exact exercises, tempo, transitions, and screening remain unknown. Gentle belly breathing, longer-exhale breathing, humming, and the sample sequence are suggested additions, **not verified contents of Amy's event**. Humming and sound-rest recommendations are primarily experience design; the cited material does not establish clinical benefits for this proposed app session.
